# 08 Clinical Safety Case

Approach: hazard analysis in the spirit of clinical risk management for health software (for example DCB0129 or ISO 14971 style reasoning; TODO verify applicability, neither is a legal requirement for this project). MindSahaay is positioned as an educational wellbeing tool, not a medical device. TODO: confirm regulatory position.

## 1. Safety claim

The system helps students notice distress and reach human help. It does not diagnose, treat, or monitor. Safety argument: (1) crisis signals always lead to human helplines; (2) the AI is constrained and never the sole safeguard; (3) limits are stated plainly to users.

## 2. Severity and likelihood scales

Severity: 1 minor, 2 moderate (distress), 3 major (delayed help), 4 catastrophic (self-harm). Likelihood: A rare, B unlikely, C possible, D likely.
Acceptable residual: severity 4 requires likelihood A and clinical advisor sign-off. By that rule HZ-19 and HZ-20 are **not acceptable** for a real deployment: their post-control likelihood is C. TODO: assign a clinical safety officer (supervisor or external psychologist).

## 4. Safety requirements derived

All controls above are in 02-srs.md. Any change touching `crisis`, `assessment` or the system prompt requires re-running the crisis evaluation and updating this log.

## 5. Limitations stated to users

The app cannot detect every crisis, is not monitored by humans in real time, and is not a substitute for professional care. Alerts reach a counsellor only inside the app and only when a counsellor signs in; there is no SMS, email, phone call or push. While your device is offline nobody is told. The screens say this in plain words. If in danger, call 112, Tele-MANAS 14416 or KIRAN 1800-599-0019.
